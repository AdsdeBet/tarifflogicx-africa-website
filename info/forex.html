More on invoicing & cross-border payments

Invoice currency: a commercial choice, not a legal requirement tied to origin

A common assumption is that an import or export invoice must be issued in the counterparty's (or the goods' country of origin's) own currency. That is not a SARB requirement — for cross-border trade, the invoicing and settlement currency is whatever the buyer and seller commercially agree to (ZAR, USD, EUR, GBP, or any other mutually agreed currency), regardless of which country the goods originate from. In practice, many South African importers and exporters invoice in USD, EUR, or GBP even when trading with a counterparty whose home currency is neither, simply because those are the most liquid, widely accepted trade currencies. The one place currency IS restricted: purely domestic invoicing between two South African residents may be issued in a foreign currency, but the actual settlement/payment must still happen in Rand.

All cross-border payments must go through an Authorised Dealer

You cannot settle an international trade payment directly, in any currency — every purchase or sale of foreign exchange by a South African resident or company must be handled by an Authorised Dealer (in practice, one of the major banks holding this SARB licence), which reports the transaction to SARB's Financial Surveillance Department regardless of the amount involved. Advance payment for imports (other than capital goods) against a commercial invoice is permitted through this channel — but the Authorised Dealer administers the specifics, so confirm your exact transaction with them before committing to payment terms in a quotation.

Keep the documentation — 5 years, same as your other customs records

Invoices, customs declarations, and transport documents supporting a cross-border payment must be retained for at least 5 years — the same retention period as the rest of your import/export documentation (see the Documentation Checklist and Customs & Excise Protocols references).

Two different exchange rates — know which one you need

This screen's live rates (refreshed daily, aggregated from a broad set of central bank and market sources covering African as well as major international currencies) are market reference rates — accurate for pricing a quotation or proforma invoice so a client can sanity-check the ZAR amount you're quoting. They are NOT the rate SARS will use when your declaration is actually processed. For a real Bill of Entry, SARS converts the invoice currency to Rand using its own "Customs Rate of Exchange" — a separate rate, sourced from SARB but published and updated by SARS itself once a day, and it can differ from live market rates. Check SARS's own published rate before finalising a customs value, not this screen's rate.
